You Have Owned The Domain For Three Years And Five Months. Nothing Has Ever Been Served On It.

0
web pages anyone can reach, anywhere

gallaghersolar.solutions does not resolve to a server. Not a holding page, not a "coming soon" — nothing.

Your email runs through that domain perfectly well. The website side of it has never been pointed at anything. Every person who has ever typed it in, or clicked it from a card or a truck, got an error.

DNS, registration and BBB records checked 18 September 2026
Finding 1 — The Exact State Of The Domain
gallaghersolar.solutions Registered: 25 April 2023 (Squarespace Domains II LLC) Expires: 25 April 2027 Nameservers: Google Cloud DNS — so it is configured, not abandoned A record (the website): none AAAA record: none CNAME: none www.gallaghersolar.solutions: does not exist MX records (email): Google Workspace — working
This is the whole finding in one line

You are paying for the domain and paying for Google Workspace, and using half of it. The email half works. The website half has never been switched on. Three years and five months of anybody who looked you up finding nothing at all.

Finding 2 — Your Email Has No Protection On It
Email authentication records on gallaghersolar.solutions: SPF (says which servers may send as you) none DKIM (signs your mail so it can be trusted) none published DMARC (tells inboxes what to do with fakes) none
Two consequences, and the second one is the expensive one
  • Your quotes are more likely to land in spam. Gmail and Outlook have tightened hard on unauthenticated mail. A proposal that never gets seen looks identical to a customer who went quiet.
  • Anybody can send email that appears to come from your domain. With no SPF or DMARC there is nothing stopping it. For a business sending quotes with dollar figures on them, that is worth closing.

All three records are free and take about twenty minutes. This is the first thing we would do, before anything else.

Finding 3 — What Actually Exists About You Online
Website none — domain does not resolve gallaghersolar.com unregistered and available southplainssolar.com unregistered — that domain is gone entirely BBB profile exists A+ rating, not accredited 0 customer reviews, 0 complaints Facebook page exists facebook.com/GallagherSolar we could not read it — Facebook required a login Meta advertising no ads found attributable to you Third-party directories several — see below
The BBB profile is the only thing you control that a customer can read

An A+ rating with zero reviews is not a criticism — it simply means the rating is not built on customer feedback yet, and a careful buyer will notice that. It is also, right now, the most substantial page about your business on the entire internet.

Two addresses, two phone numbers, and one outright false claim
  • The street numbers disagree — 8502 against 8402 — and so do the phone numbers.
  • At least one directory states that your business "maintains an active website with service detail". That is simply false — there is no website. These pages are auto-generated and nobody checks them.
  • When a search engine sees your name, address and phone stated differently in different places, it trusts all of them less. This is the one part of local search that is genuinely mechanical, and it is fixable.

We are not telling you which is correct — you are. But right now the internet is publishing two versions of your business and you have no page of your own to settle it.

Finding 5 — Every Job Currently Depends On Somebody Else's Goodwill
What you told us
  • You could take five to ten more installs with what you have now.
  • You are doing $500,000 to $700,000 and want to roughly double that in a year or two.
  • Your work comes through roofers, plus referrals and repeat customers.
  • You have worked with lead generation before.
The risk nobody mentions until it happens

A referral pipeline is a good pipeline right up until the referrer changes their mind. One of the solar firms in Lubbock already sells roofing alongside solar — which means the roofers you rely on are exactly the relationship a competitor has a reason to go after. If two of your roofers go elsewhere, you have no second channel and no page for anyone to find.

The point of a website here is not vanity. It is that a roofer can send someone a link and look good doing it, and that some jobs arrive without a middleman taking a cut.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the email records first. SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Free, twenty minutes, and it protects every quote you send from here on.
  • Point the domain at an actual site — and decide whether to pick up gallaghersolar.com while it is still available, because a .com is easier to say down a phone.
  • Build the page a roofer can forward. Your work, your finance options, what happens after the install, and a form that captures the roof and the bill before you drive out.
  • Settle the address and the phone number in one place, then correct the directories against it.
  • Put the meter on before any spend, so when we do go after volume, you can see which jobs came from where — which is the part that was missing last time you tried lead generation.

Five to ten more installs a month, and nowhere for anyone to land.

The crew, the trade and the referrals already work. The front door has never been built.
